01 — Overview

What TrendLens does

The innovation team receives trend reports from multiple agencies every month. TrendLens ingests all of them, measures real consumer demand via Google Trends, harvests the open web, and synthesises everything into one navigable system — so no insight gets buried in a PDF nobody re-reads.

🔗
Everything is traceable.

Any claim in TrendLens resolves to a specific page in a specific report, or to a specific external URL. When you present a stat to leadership, you can open the original source in one click.

02 — Data Sources

What goes in

Four types of sources, all stored in Google BigQuery.

📊 Agency decks

PPTX and PDF trend reports from external agencies. Uploaded manually through the dashboard and extracted into taxonomy trees.

📘 Mintel reports

Downloaded from the Mintel portal, tagged so Mintel-specific insights can be surfaced separately on their own page.

🟢 Tyson monthly digests

Tyson's own internal trend insight cards. Cross-document themes are clustered from these and shown on the Tyson Bites page.

🌐 Web harvest

Open internet — news articles, brand launches, food blogs. Harvested per megatrend to validate signals exist outside agency reports.

Also in BigQuery

tyson_products_enriched

~6,300 Tyson SKUs from Salsify with clean consumer-facing product names, category/segment, and full ingredient lists. Used to surface "what Tyson already makes" against each megatrend.

Google Trends (Playwright scraping)

Real-browser scraping measures year-over-year demand for every trend term. No API key required — runs locally against the real Google Trends interface.

05 — Trust

The citation system

Every synthesised claim carries a citation. Two kinds:

📄

Internal document

Links to an uploaded PDF or PPTX and opens it at the exact page number where the claim was found. Used for agency decks, Mintel reports, and Tyson digests.

📄 Mintel Protein Trends 2025· p.14
↗

External URL

Links to a web article, brand press release, or news source. Opens in a new tab. Used for web harvest finds and external validation.

↗ Chipotle High-Protein Menu Launch — Chipotle.com
🔢
Academic-style numbering:

Superscripts [1] [2] [3] appear inline next to claims. The same source used multiple times gets the same number throughout the dossier. All resolve in the References section at the bottom.

06 — Key Concepts

Things to know

Whitespace vs. Opportunity — the difference

Whitespace

A market gap nobody has claimed yet — including Tyson. Formats proven in other markets (Japan, Korea, Europe) that haven't landed in the US. The signal.

"Konbini egg salad sandwiches are selling at every Japanese 7-Eleven and just starting to appear in US c-stores — nobody in the US frozen aisle owns this."
Opportunity

A specific Tyson product concept scoped to existing manufacturing, brands, and categories — with a tier and reasoning. The response.

"Konbini Karaage Grab-and-Go Packs — chilled 3-piece fried chicken for the c-store case. Adjacent tier — Tyson has the capability, not the format."
💬
One-liner: Whitespace is the signal ("there's a gap here"). Opportunity is the response ("here's how Tyson fills it").

The three permission tiers

Core
Tyson already makes this.

The product type exists in the portfolio. This is a renovation, re-positioning, or line extension — not a new build. Lowest risk, fastest to market. Example: adding a hot honey glaze to an existing bacon line.

Adjacent
New format, existing brand permission.

Tyson has the brand equity and manufacturing capability — it's a new occasion, format, or flavor system. Medium risk, medium investment. Example: Tyson launching a konbini-style karaage pack for c-stores.

Stretch
Genuinely new territory.

Requires new capability, a partnership, or a new brand build. High reward, high investment. Example: self-heating packaging (exothermic element), freeze-dried fried chicken at scale.

How megatrend strength is measured

Four signals: Report classes (how many source types mention it), Report count (total docs), Month coverage (consecutive monthly digests — durability), Rising terms (Google Trends YoY growth).
